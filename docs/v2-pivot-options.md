# Presence — Adoption Pivots & Expanded Utility

How to make Presence dramatically easier to adopt and more useful to everyday relationship
health and happiness — without abandoning the core idea (presence, listening, nudges) or the
moat (`v2-strategy-7-powers.md`).

---

## 1. The adoption problem, named honestly

The current concept asks a couple to do the single hardest thing first:

> place a phone between you, **both** opt in, and **record a live — often charged —
> conversation**, then receive a debrief.

Every clause is friction:

- **Two-player from step one.** The most resistant partner vetoes the whole product. This is
  the worst possible cold start, and (per the strategy doc) it's a *power* risk, not just a
  growth one — the moat can't compound if both partners never get in.
- **Recording a hard conversation is high-stakes** and changes behavior (observer effect). It
  can feel like wearing a wire to a fight.
- **Episodic and low-frequency.** It only fires during real conversations, so it never
  becomes a habit and the memory corpus accrues slowly.
- **Crisis-coded.** It reads as "couples-therapy adjacent" — something you reach for when
  things are bad — which shrinks the market and raises the emotional bar to start.
- **Delayed value**, and the live nudge (the boldest part) is also the least proven.

The fix is not to abandon the live-listening vision — it's the most differentiated thing
Presence has. The fix is to **stop making it the front door.**

---

## 2. The reframe

From a *conversation analyzer for couples in conflict* (clinical, two-player, episodic) to a
**relationship companion that helps one person show up better, builds a light daily/weekly
habit, and pulls the partner in over time** (everyday, preventive, solo-friendly, joyful).

Same mission, same behavioral science, much lower bar — and a far larger market
("relationship wellness" >> "couples-therapy tool").

This also matches the brief's own ethos: optimize for flourishing and the joy loop, not
crisis and surveillance.

---

## 3. Pivot menu

### A. Lower the bar — who, when, how

**A1. Single-player on-ramp (the biggest unlock).** Let one partner start *alone*: reflect on
the relationship, prep for a hard talk, process one that already happened, practice a daily
habit. One motivated person gets real value solo, builds trust, and *invites* the other once
it's earned. Kills the two-player cold start; the dyadic lock-in still forms later.

**A2. Async / bring-your-own-conversation.** Drop the requirement to record live. Let users
*recount* what happened, paste a text thread, or leave a voice memo. Lower stakes, higher
frequency, no "wire to a fight." Live recording becomes one input, not the only one.

**A3. Demote live "presence" listening to an advanced couples mode.** Keep it as the
aspirational north of the product — something bought-in couples opt into — not the entry
point.

### B. Add everyday utility — frequency + health & happiness

**B1. Pre-conversation prep (priming).** "We need to talk about money — help me go in well."
Clarify what you need, anticipate their needs, frame a soft start-up. Proactive (prevents
disconnection rather than autopsying it), single-player, high-utility.

**B2. Decoder / translator on demand (the viral wedge).** "They said *X* and I don't know what
they meant or how to respond." Recount → NVC translation (what may have been meant, the unmet
need) + a suggested non-escalating response. Lowest-friction, single-player, immediate
utility, intensely word-of-mouth ("this stopped me blowing up").

**B3. Repair assistant.** After a rupture: a guided reconnection, a soft re-entry message, a
reflection. The moment of highest pain is the moment of highest willingness to adopt.

**B4. Daily/weekly micro-rituals & guided check-ins.** Relationship *fitness*, not therapy: a
daily appreciation, a 2-minute check-in, a weekly "how are we" retro. Builds the habit and
the memory corpus, joyful not clinical, works solo or together.

**B5. Shared relationship memory / OS.** A warm shared space for the couple's agreements,
values, important dates, recurring issues, wins, and gratitude — both contribute. This is the
Switching-Costs artifact and adds standing utility between conversations.

### C. Reframe the category

Position as a **relationship wellness companion** (everyday, preventive, solo-friendly), not a
conversation analyzer or couples-therapy tool. Bigger TAM, lower emotional bar, on-brand.

---

## 4. Recommended path: land-and-expand

Don't pick one pivot — sequence them into a funnel that fixes the cold start while preserving
the vision.

1. **Land (solo, low-friction):** decoder (B2) + pre-talk prep (B1) + post-talk processing
   (A2) + a daily micro-ritual (B4). One partner, immediate utility, no recording, no buy-in
   from the other required. This is the wedge.
2. **Habit:** light weekly check-in (B4) builds frequency and starts the memory corpus.
3. **Invite the partner:** once value and trust are felt, bring the second person into shared
   rituals and the shared memory/OS (B5). The dyadic lock-in forms here — *after* value, not
   before it.
4. **Expand (couples, advanced):** the live "presence" listening + in-the-moment nudge (A3)
   becomes the opt-in payoff for bought-in couples — the original vision, now reached by
   couples who already trust the product.

**Highest-leverage single pivot:** the **solo decoder/prep wedge (A1 + B1 + B2)**. It removes
the two-player barrier, delivers value in the first session with no recording, and seeds trust
+ data before the partner is ever involved.

---

## 5. Why this *strengthens* the moat (not just growth)

| Pivot | Effect on the 7 Powers |
|---|---|
| Solo on-ramp (A1) | Brand + Cornered-Resource data accrue from one user → fixes the dyadic cold start that throttles every power |
| Daily rituals (B4) | Frequency → more consented data (Cornered Resource) + habit (Switching Costs) + joy (Brand) |
| Decoder / prep (B1/B2) | High-utility wedge → word-of-mouth (Brand, referral) |
| Shared memory/OS (B5) | The Switching-Costs artifact, now standing not episodic |
| Async (A2) | More sessions → faster-compounding memory and dataset |

Easier adoption is how the moat *starts*: a single trusted solo user is the first deposit in
the brand, the dataset, and (once the partner joins) the switching-cost corpus.

---

## 6. Tensions & guardrails

- **Daily habit ≠ engagement bait.** Frequency must come from genuine ritual value, never
  streaks, guilt, or notification nags — the same line the strategy and joy-loop docs draw.
  Health-and-happiness utility justifies coming back; dopamine mechanics don't.
- **Solo mode must stay two-sided in spirit.** Helping one partner can't become arming one
  side against the other. Keep the NVC frame: translate toward *understanding and the other's
  unmet need*, never toward winning. This protects the Brand (never-blame) that the whole
  moat rests on.
- **Don't lose the differentiator.** The live presence/nudge is the boldest, most defensible
  vision — demote it as the *front door*, but keep it on the roadmap as the expand-stage
  payoff, or Presence becomes just another journaling/coaching app.
- **Prove the invention still holds.** Whatever the front door, the north-star test is
  unchanged: did two humans understand each other better? Solo utility must ladder up to that,
  not replace it.

---

## 7. The one line

Keep the vision, move the front door: **lead with a solo, everyday relationship companion
(decode, prep, reflect, small rituals) that one partner adopts with almost no friction — then
expand into shared rituals, shared memory, and finally the live presence mode for couples who
already trust it.** Easier adoption and a stronger moat turn out to be the same move.
