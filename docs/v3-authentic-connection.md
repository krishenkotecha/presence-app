# Presence — The Authentic-Connection Thesis (Matchmaker as a Service)

How the core pain and product extend to a second, deeper need — **authentic connection,
with or without dating apps** — and what "matchmaker as a service" means strategically.

Builds on `v3-direction.md` (core pain), `v3-lifecycle.md` (stage map), and
`v2-strategy-7-powers.md` (moat).

---

## 1. The reframe

Presence is not a couples tool or a dating app. It is an **authentic-connection company**
with **one core asset** and **two product expressions**:

- **Core asset:** a high-fidelity, behaviorally-learned model of *how a person relates* —
  their communication style, attachment patterns, what they actually need, how they show up.
  Everything the product does (decode / prep / reflect / the recurring-theme memory) exists
  to build this model.
- **Expression A — matchmaking** (front of the lifecycle): use the model to *introduce* you
  to people you'd authentically connect with.
- **Expression B — relationship-deepening** (rest of the lifecycle): use the model to help
  you *understand* the person you're with.

Both serve one meta-need underneath the said-vs-meant pain: **to be truly known, and to find
people worth knowing.** Matchmaking is the same engine pointed one stage earlier.

---

## 2. Why matchmaking is a *better* version of the same idea

- **Better matching data.** Dating apps match on stated preferences and photos —
  performative and aspirational (people lie, even to themselves). Presence matches on
  *behavioral, reflective truth* learned from a user's own reflections. A fundamentally
  better compatibility substrate — and it is the consented **Cornered-Resource** dataset from
  the 7 Powers doc, now doing double duty.
- **The strongest form of the counter-position.** Dating apps profit from *engagement* — they
  win when you don't find lasting connection and churn back to swiping. A matchmaker that
  profits from *successful, lasting connection* (you graduate and leave) is something the
  swipe economy structurally cannot copy without breaking its own model. "Authentic
  connection with or without dating apps" *is* that position.
- **A real network economy — finally.** The 7 Powers analysis flagged network effects as weak
  for the couples product. Matchmaking is inherently two-sided: a genuine network moat the
  current product lacks.

---

## 3. The honest risks (a harder, different business)

- **Liquidity cold-start.** Matchmaking needs a pool; a coaching-app userbase isn't
  automatically a dating pool. Two-sided liquidity is the graveyard of dating startups — the
  network economy is the opportunity *and* the chief execution risk.
- **Positioning dilution.** "Understanding coach" and "matchmaker" are different jobs; do both
  poorly and you're neither.
- **Privacy stakes jump.** Using someone's intimate reflections to *match* them is far
  higher-stakes than coaching them. The trust keystone becomes even more load-bearing;
  consent architecture is existential.
- **Brutal market.** Match Group dominance, high CAC, fraud and safety — table stakes in
  dating.

---

## 4. The sequencing that makes it credible

Don't launch matchmaking cold — let it *emerge* from a base of users who already trust you
with their self-model, because matching is only differentiated once that model is deep.

1. **Layer on top of the dating apps ("with dating apps").** Start as pure current-product:
   decode your matches' messages, prep your dates, reflect after. This meets users where they
   already are, delivers immediate value, and quietly accumulates the self-model.
2. **Deepen the self-model + trust** through everyday use (the §1 asset compounds).
3. **Become the alternative underneath ("without dating apps").** Once you hold a rich,
   trusted self-model for enough people, offer Presence-native introductions on *behavioral
   compatibility*, monetized on connection rather than engagement.

> Parasitize the apps, then replace them. The wedge is being a helpful layer on the apps; the
> destination is being the apps' alternative.

---

## 5. The connection loop

The funnel closes into a single loop, with the self-model powering every step:

> reflect / understand → know yourself and what you need → get introduced to someone genuinely
> compatible → Presence coaches the early connection (back to decode / prep) → the bond deepens
> (the memory / couples product) → richer self-model → better introductions.

Authentic connection is the through-line; matchmaking closes the lifecycle loop to the *left*
and feeds users back into the relationship product.

---

## 6. How it maps to the moat

- **Self-model as matching substrate** → deepens the **Cornered Resource** (behavioral,
  consented data no dating app has).
- **Profit-on-connection model** → the sharpest **Counter-Positioning** (vs the engagement-
  driven swipe economy).
- **Two-sided introductions** → a genuine **Network Economy** (the previously-weak power).
- **Compounding self-model + relationship memory** → **Switching Costs** (your connection
  history lives here).
- All of it rests on **trust** — the keystone — which the matchmaking use raises the stakes on.

---

## 7. The integrity guardrail (non-negotiable)

The same principle that forced "scaffold, not script" applies double here. A matchmaker built
on a behavioral self-model must serve the user's *authentic* connection — never optimize a
manipulable "success" metric, and never nudge people toward whoever maximizes platform
revenue. The moment it does, it becomes the thing it counter-positions against. Profit must
stay aligned to lasting connection, or the entire thesis collapses into another swipe app.

---

## 8. The one line, and the beachhead

> **Presence helps you connect authentically — first by helping you understand yourself and
> others, then by introducing you to people you'd actually click with.**

Beachhead discipline (from `v3-lifecycle.md`): don't try to span the whole lifecycle or launch
matchmaking on day one. Win the understanding product first; the self-model it accumulates is
the only thing that makes the matchmaking differentiated rather than another dating app with a
coaching skin.

**Disconfirming test:** if introductions built on the self-model don't produce *more lasting*
connections than swipe-based matching, the core thesis — that behavioral self-knowledge beats
stated preferences — is wrong, and matchmaking is a distraction from the relationship product.
