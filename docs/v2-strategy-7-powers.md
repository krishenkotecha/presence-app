# Presence — Strategy through Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers

A power is *the set of conditions creating the potential for persistent differential
returns.* Helmer's test is strict: every Power must have both a **Benefit** (it improves
cash flow — higher prices, lower costs, lower churn, lower CAC) **and** a **Barrier** (a
reason competitors do not, or cannot, compete it away). Benefit without Barrier gets
arbitraged; Barrier without Benefit earns nothing.

This document maps Presence against all seven, honestly (including the ones that don't
apply), and then sequences a route to power so the app becomes a durable business — not
just a good demo.

---

## 0. The honest starting point

**The technology is not the moat.** Transcription, diarization, and an LLM that applies
Gottman/NVC framing are all rentable or replicable within a quarter. If Presence's edge is
"better analysis," it has Benefit and no Barrier — it will be copied.

The invention worth protecting is different: *an in-the-moment, privacy-first relationship
aid that measurably helps two specific people understand each other, and that gets more
valuable the longer they use it.* The durable power lives in **the compounding, consented
relational memory of each couple** — and the trust required to hold it. Everything below
routes through that.

## 2. The strong powers (build the strategy here)

### Switching Costs — *primary, and the most buildable*

Helmer's three switching-cost types all stack for Presence:

- **Procedural / data:** the product literally accumulates an irreplaceable corpus — *this*
  couple's patterns, history, remembered moments, "what's improving." Leaving means losing
  your relationship's memory and starting from zero.
- **Relational:** the couple forms an emotional attachment to the artifact (the moments
  timeline, the "you tried it and it worked" callbacks). You don't abandon the keeper of
  your good moments lightly.
- **Dyadic lock-in:** it takes *two* people to adopt and *two* to leave. A competitor must
  re-win both partners simultaneously.

**Benefit:** low churn → high LTV; pricing power for a subscription.
**Barrier:** a rival can copy the features but not the years of *your* accumulated memory.

**Moves:** ship the longitudinal relational model and the **moments timeline** early (the
"joyful longitudinal" from the redesign doc); make memory visible and emotionally precious;
let users export but make the in-app continuity the thing they'd hate to lose; design the
"you tried it, and it worked" loop so value is obviously cumulative, not per-session.

> Strategic timing: switching costs only count if established *before* competitive intensity
> rises. The corpus must start compounding now, while acquisition is cheap.

### Counter-Positioning — *the entry wedge*

The incumbents — engagement/subscription relationship apps (Paired, Lasting, Relish) and any
data-monetizing platform — run business models Presence can deliberately invert. The brief's
own constraints ("do NOT optimize for screen time, engagement, notifications"; "privacy
first") are not just ethics; they are a **counter-position**:

- An ad- or engagement-driven incumbent **cannot** copy a product designed to *reduce* app
  time and send users back to each other — doing so cannibalizes its own model.
- A data-monetizing platform **cannot** credibly copy "we don't monetize your intimate
  conversations / on-device option" without repudiating its revenue.

**Benefit:** a differentiated, trustworthy entry that the obvious competitors won't chase.
**Barrier:** not capability — *self-harm.* The incumbent's existing business punishes
imitation (Helmer's defining feature of counter-positioning).

**Moves:** make the anti-engagement, privacy-first stance loud and structural (on-device
processing option, transparent deletion, no notification-nag). It both wins trust and dares
incumbents into a copy that hurts them.

### Branding — *the durable B2C moat, built slowly*

Helmer's two brand mechanisms — **uncertainty reduction** and **affective valence** — are
maximally relevant here. People are handing over their most private moments; "will this be
safe? will it judge me?" is exactly uncertainty, and "this feels caring, hopeful, on our
side" is exactly affect. A brand that reliably delivers *safe, warm, non-judgmental, it
actually helped us* commands premium and love.

**Benefit:** price premium + referral-driven, lower CAC.
**Barrier:** brand in a trust category is earned over years of consistent, non-creepy,
two-sided behavior — it can't be bought or rushed.

**Moves:** ruthless consistency on the §6 guardrails (never blame a partner, never fabricate
precision, never surveil). Every review that feels fair and warm is a brand deposit; one
that feels like grading or spying is a withdrawal.

## 3. The situational power (worth manufacturing)

### Cornered Resource — *the consented outcome-labeled dataset*

A genuinely coveted, hard-to-acquire asset Presence is uniquely positioned to build: a corpus
of **real couple conversations, consented, paired with outcome labels** — the usefulness
signal ("did this help you understand each other?") already instrumented at the row level,
tied to model + confidence.

**Benefit:** trains a relational-intelligence model competitors can't match → better product
→ reinforces brand and switching costs (a flywheel).
**Barrier:** access. You cannot buy or scrape intimate, consented, outcome-labeled
relationship data — it requires trust + scale, which only the brand + privacy stance unlock.
The barrier is *the very trust the other powers create.*

**Moves:** treat the feedback loop as a strategic asset, not a metric; build the
consent/data-rights architecture so this corpus is both ethical and proprietary;
optionally secure cornered *talent* too (named relationship-science advisors) for
credibility and brand.

> Helmer caution: this is only a true Cornered Resource if access stays genuinely hard. Keep
> the trust/privacy bar high enough that the data can't be commoditized.

---

## 4. The weak / late powers (be honest, don't over-invest)

**Network Economies — weak.** Relationships are dyadic, so the "network" is mostly a 2-node
lock-in (counted under switching costs). Across couples there's no classic effect — your
insight doesn't improve because another couple joined, except indirectly via the dataset
(that's Cornered Resource/learning, which Helmer distinguishes from true network economies).
Real but limited upside: referral (happy couples → other couples) and a **therapist
channel** (clinicians recommending it between sessions). Pursue those as distribution, not as
a network moat.

**Scale Economies — weak.** Fixed model/dev cost amortized over users gives mild leverage,
but inference is rentable and the category isn't winner-take-all on cost. Don't build the
thesis here.

**Process Power — late-stage.** A hard-to-replicate organizational capability for producing
*safe, useful, non-judgmental* nudges at scale could eventually become Process Power (à la
Toyota), but it requires years of embedded practice. It's a stability-stage reward, not an
origination play.

---

## 5. The route to power (sequenced by stage)

Helmer: different powers are *establishable* at different industry stages. Presence's path:

**Origination (now — the MVP):**
- Lead with **Counter-Positioning** (privacy-first, anti-engagement) to enter against
  incumbents who won't follow.
- Begin accruing the **Cornered-Resource** dataset from day one (the consented usefulness
  signal is the seed).
- Validate the invention itself (Steve Blank): do two humans understand each other better?

**Takeoff (growth):**
- Compound **Switching Costs** — the relational memory / moments timeline — *before*
  competitive intensity rises. This is the decisive window.
- Establish **Branding** through relentless consistency on trust and warmth.
- Use referral + therapist channels for distribution.

**Stability (scale):**
- Convert accumulated practice into **Process Power**; deepen the model from the cornered
  dataset.

The throughline: *the joy loop and the privacy stance are not just product choices — they
are the engine that manufactures four of the seven powers* (switching costs, counter-
positioning, branding, cornered resource). Strategy and product are the same bet.

---

## 6. How the powers become a business

- **Pricing:** switching costs + brand support a couples subscription (and a therapist/B2B2C
  tier) rather than ad revenue — which also *preserves* the counter-position.
- **Churn:** the compounding memory is the anti-churn mechanism; LTV rises with tenure.
- **CAC:** brand + dyadic referral + therapist recommendations lower acquisition cost over
  time.
- **Reinvestment:** the cornered dataset → better model → stronger brand → more consented
  data. The flywheel is the business.

## 7. The central claim

If only one sentence survives: **Presence wins not by analyzing conversations better, but by
becoming the trusted, private keeper of a couple's relational memory — an asset that
compounds (switching costs), that ad/data incumbents structurally can't copy
(counter-positioning), that earns love in the most trust-sensitive category there is
(branding), and that quietly accrues a dataset no one else can ethically obtain (cornered
resource).** Build those four; treat scale, network, and process as bonuses, not the plan.

---

## 8. Risks & disconfirming tests

- **No invention, no power.** If the MVP can't show couples understand each other better, no
  power matters. Test that first (the usefulness signal is the instrument).
- **Trust is the load-bearing wall.** A single privacy breach or a "creepy/judgmental"
  moment doesn't dent the brand — it can collapse the cornered resource *and* the switching
  costs at once, because all three rest on trust. Guardrails are existential, not cosmetic.
- **Switching costs can feel like a cage.** If users feel locked in rather than cared for,
  the relational switching cost inverts into resentment. Keep export honest and the value
  obviously cumulative, so staying is a choice, not a trap.
- **Counter-positioning has a clock.** It's an entry wedge, not a permanent moat —
  incumbents eventually accept the model or new entrants copy it. Use the window to bank the
  durable powers (switching costs, brand).

## 9. Strengthening the position

The four powers above are the floor. To make them *durable* rather than merely present,
deepen them into a reinforcing system.

**Stack the powers into one flywheel.** The strongest positions aren't four separate moats —
they're a loop: *trust (brand) → unlocks consent/data rights others can't get → trains a
better relational model → deepens per-couple personalization → raises switching costs →
strengthens brand.* Design every feature to feed **two** powers at once (the "you tried it,
and it worked" callback already feeds Switching Costs + joy).

**Trust is the keystone — over-invest in it.** It sits under three of the four powers
(Counter-Positioning, Branding, Cornered Resource), so a single breach can collapse three
moats at once, and conversely *one* unit of trust strengthens three. Make it verifiable, not
asserted: a third-party privacy audit, open-sourcing the on-device pipeline, and explicit
never-blame content guarantees turn "trust us" into a barrier rivals must match in kind.

**Make switching costs *generative*, not archival.** Today leaving means "lose our history."
Stronger: the longer a couple is in, the better the model understands *them*, so leaving
degrades the tool's quality — not just loses an archive. Add co-authored artifacts (shared
agreements, values, rituals) the couple returns to, weaving the product into the
relationship's operating rhythm.

**Chase the scarce data: longitudinal outcomes.** "Was this useful?" is a good seed, but the
truly un-buyable Cornered Resource is *outcome-labeled* data — did satisfaction rise, did they
stay together. It can't be scraped; it requires trust + time, which only an incumbent of
trust will have. Pair it with a **named clinical/scientific partnership** (a Gottman-caliber
advisory board or co-developed instrument): a Cornered Resource *and* a brand accelerator.

**Tighten the incumbent's bind; turn therapists into a channel.** Counter-Positioning is
strongest when the incumbent's economics actively punish imitation — so lean into a model
that depends on *not* monetizing data and *not* maximizing engagement (on-device default;
pricing aligned to "we profit only if your relationship improves"). And partner *with*
therapists rather than competing: it's distribution, and it deepens dyadic lock-in to triadic
(three parties to move, not two).

**Compress the slow brand build with borrowed credibility.** Brand in a trust category is
slow; accelerate with an efficacy study and a clinical advisory board (uncertainty reduction
at speed) plus the verifiable privacy posture above.

**The latent network economy.** Across couples the effect is weak, but a two-sided
**therapist ↔ couple network** (clinicians bring couples; couples attract clinicians) is a
real network economy the consumer-only design leaves on the table — a candidate later wedge.

### The three highest-leverage bets

1. **Trust as keystone** — verifiable privacy + never-blame guarantees (strengthens three
   powers at once).
2. **Longitudinal outcome dataset** + a named clinical partnership (the un-buyable Cornered
   Resource).
3. **Therapist channel** — distribution today, triadic lock-in and a two-sided network later.

### Adoption is a power risk, not just a growth problem

The biggest structural weakness here is the **dyadic cold start** — power can't compound if
couples never get both partners in. The pivots in `v2-pivot-options.md` (especially a
single-player on-ramp) directly de-risk the powers: they let Branding and the Cornered-
Resource dataset begin accruing from *one* motivated partner, before the second ever joins.
Easier adoption isn't separate from the moat — it's how the moat gets started.
